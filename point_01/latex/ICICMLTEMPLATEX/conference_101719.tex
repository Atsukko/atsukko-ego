\begin{abstract}
Egocentric 3D human pose estimation aims to recover accurate and temporally stable body poses from head-mounted sensors, yet remains challenging due to severe self-occlusion, limited field of view, and rapid motion changes. Although recent methods leverage transformer-based temporal modeling to capture long-range dependencies, they largely rely on implicit learning and often fail to explicitly enforce anatomical joint constraints and joint-wise local temporal consistency inherent in human pose dynamics. In this paper, we propose an enhanced spatiotemporal framework that explicitly strengthens both spatial and temporal representations for egocentric 3D pose estimation. On the spatial side, we introduce a Learnable Graph Convolution (LGC) module that integrates human skeletal anatomical priors with data-driven joint relationships, enabling adaptive modeling of inter-joint dependencies while preserving precise 3D joint coordinates. On the temporal side, we propose a Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing (JATS) module that performs lightweight local temporal refinement independently for each joint-coordinate channel through depth-wise temporal filtering and learnable joint-wise and coordinate-aware gating, explicitly enhancing short-term temporal consistency while preserving joint-specific motion patterns. By jointly incorporating these two modules before global temporal reasoning, the proposed framework provides cleaner and more coherent spatiotemporal features for subsequent transformer-based modeling. Extensive experiments on the SELF dataset demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms multiple competitive methods in terms of MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, and 3D-PCK, while introducing only marginal computational overhead and maintaining real-time inference performance, validating the effectiveness of explicitly disentangling and reinforcing spatial anatomical structure and joint-aware local temporal dynamics for egocentric 3D human pose estimation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Egocentric 3D human pose estimation aims to recover full-body 3D poses from head-mounted cameras and sensors, and has recently attracted increasing attention due to its wide applications in virtual reality, augmented reality, and human–computer interaction. By placing sensors on the wearer, egocentric systems enable unconstrained motion capture without relying on external cameras, offering strong practicality in real-world scenarios. However, estimating accurate and temporally stable poses from an egocentric viewpoint remains highly challenging, as large portions of the body are frequently occluded, observations are often incomplete, viewpoints change rapidly with head motion, and pose dynamics can be highly complex.

To address these challenges, recent methods have explored transformer-based architectures to model long-range temporal dependencies in pose sequences. Prior work has shown that global temporal attention can effectively capture long-term pose evolution and improve robustness under severe occlusions. Nevertheless, these approaches predominantly rely on implicit learning to capture both spatial joint relationships and temporal dynamics. As a result, explicit anatomical joint constraints and joint-wise local temporal consistency are not directly enforced, which may limit spatial coherence and temporal stability in challenging egocentric scenarios.

Egocentric 3D human pose estimation exhibits strong structural regularities in both space and time. Spatially, body joints follow well-defined anatomical connectivity governed by kinematic constraints, where explicitly modeling inter-joint dependencies can significantly improve spatial coherence. Temporally, pose trajectories are locally smooth, while different joints exhibit distinct temporal characteristics: some joints remain relatively stable, whereas others undergo rapid and irregular movements. These observations suggest that explicitly strengthening anatomical joint relationships and enforcing joint-wise local temporal consistency can provide a more reliable foundation for subsequent global temporal reasoning.

Motivated by this insight, we build upon a strong transformer-based egocentric pose estimation framework and propose an enhanced spatiotemporal architecture that explicitly reinforces both spatial and temporal representations, while being carefully designed to introduce only minimal additional computational overhead. On the spatial side, we introduce a Learnable Graph Convolution (LGC) module that integrates fixed skeleton-based anatomical priors with a learnable graph structure, allowing the model to balance predefined kinematic relationships and data-driven joint dependencies. On the temporal side, we propose a Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing module, which performs lightweight local temporal refinement by applying learnable, joint-specific residual smoothing along the temporal dimension. Instead of relying on heavy temporal modeling, this module explicitly enforces short-term temporal consistency while preserving joint-specific motion patterns, providing cleaner and more stable temporal features before global transformer-based temporal modeling.

Extensive experiments on the SELF dataset demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms multiple existing methods across standard evaluation metrics, including MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, and 3D-PCK, while maintaining real-time inference efficiency. Comprehensive ablation studies further validate the complementary roles of the proposed spatial and temporal components, highlighting the effectiveness of explicitly disentangling and strengthening spatial structure and joint-aware local temporal modeling.

In summary, our main contributions are threefold:

We introduce a Learnable Graph Convolution (LGC) module that explicitly enhances spatial joint relationships by integrating anatomical priors with data-driven graph learning.

We propose a Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing(JATS) module that strengthens local temporal consistency through learnable, joint-specific residual refinement, serving as a lightweight temporal enhancement prior to transformer-based reasoning.

We achieve competitive performance on egocentric 3D human pose estimation benchmarks compared with multiple existing methods, using an efficient and unified spatiotemporal framework.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Egocentric 3D Human Pose Estimation}
Recent research on egocentric 3D human pose estimation has achieved remarkable progress in both accuracy and efficiency. FRAME~\cite{b1} leverages geometric multimodal integration and floor-aligned representations to achieve state-of-the-art performance with real-time inference. REWIND~\cite{b2} employs cascaded diffusion-based denoising and identity-conditioned refinement to enable high-fidelity whole-body pose estimation in real time. Wang et al.~\cite{b3} introduce FisheyeViT combined with diffusion-based motion refinement to obtain temporally stable pose estimates from a single head-mounted fisheye camera.

Beyond vision-only approaches, multimodal systems have also been explored. Dai et al.~\cite{b4} propose the SLOPER4D dataset by integrating LiDAR and IMUs, facilitating global 4D human pose estimation in large-scale outdoor environments. Wang et al.~\cite{b5} adopt a spatio-temporal optimization framework with learned motion priors to reduce temporal jitter and address occlusions in global-coordinate egocentric pose estimation. Akada et al.~\cite{b6} introduce the UnrealEgo dataset and a multi-branch autoencoder architecture for stereo egocentric pose estimation, explicitly addressing self-occlusion via heatmap-driven modeling. Building upon this, Akada et al.~\cite{b7} further propose a transformer-based framework that integrates scene depth features and temporal query augmentation, along with the UnrealEgo2 dataset, to improve robustness in dynamic environments. EgoPoseFormer~\cite{b8} presents a two-stage transformer-based framework with pose proposal and deformable stereo attention for accurate stereo egocentric 3D pose refinement.

Despite these advances, most existing egocentric approaches primarily rely on global temporal modeling or implicit learning of joint relationships, while explicit modeling of anatomical structure and local temporal consistency remains limited.

\subsection{General 3D Human Pose Estimation}
General 3D human pose estimation aims to recover 3D joint coordinates from monocular or multi-view images and videos, and has been extensively studied. KTPFormer~\cite{b9} introduces a transformer enhanced with kinematic and trajectory priors, employing graph convolution to enforce anatomical constraints and temporal coherence. MotionAGFormer~\cite{b10} proposes an adaptive graph attention transformer that dynamically learns joint dependencies through multi-scale graph convolutions and temporal attention. TCPFormer~\cite{b11} presents a temporal correlation learning framework using implicit pose proxies to facilitate comprehensive sequence-level temporal modeling.

Other works explore alternative architectures for structural and temporal modeling. GraphMLP~\cite{b12} combines multi-layer perceptrons with graph convolution to capture global and local joint interactions efficiently. Cheng et al.~\cite{b13} employ temporal convolutional networks together with a Cylinder Man Model to explicitly handle occlusions and enforce temporal smoothness in video-based pose estimation. PyMAF-X~\cite{b14} extends parametric human mesh recovery by incorporating pyramidal mesh alignment feedback and adaptive integration strategies for full-body regression from monocular images.

While these methods demonstrate the effectiveness of graph-based modeling and temporal reasoning, they are primarily designed for third-person settings and often rely on heavy temporal architectures. In contrast, our work focuses on egocentric scenarios and emphasizes explicitly strengthening spatial anatomical relations and joint-adaptive local temporal consistency as a lightweight and complementary enhancement to transformer-based temporal modeling.

\section{Method}
Our approach is built upon the FRAME~\cite{b1} architecture. We retain its backbone network for feature extraction and 3D joint coordinate regression, as well as the original transformer encoder for global temporal modeling. To explicitly enhance spatial structure and local temporal consistency prior to transformer-based reasoning, we insert two lightweight modules before the transformer encoder: a Learnable Graph Convolution (LGC) module for spatial refinement, followed by a Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing (JATS) module for local temporal enhancement.











\subsection{Learnable Graph Convolution}\label{AA}
To explicitly model structured spatial relationships among human body joints, we introduce a Learnable Graph Convolution (LGC) module as a spatial refinement stage prior to temporal modeling. The LGC module enhances joint representations by integrating anatomical priors with data-driven joint dependencies, while preserving the original 3D coordinate information through a residual design.

Given input joint features $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times V \times J \times 3}$, where $B$ denotes the batch size, $T$ the temporal length, $V$ the number of views, and $J$ the number of joints, the input is first reshaped into a graph-compatible format $\mathbf{X}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{(B \cdot T \cdot V) \times J \times 3}$.

The core of the LGC module is a hybrid adjacency matrix that combines fixed anatomical connectivity with a learnable joint relationship matrix. Specifically, a fixed adjacency matrix $\mathbf{A}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times J}$ is constructed based on the kinematic tree of the human skeleton. For a set of $J=15$ joints, undirected edges are defined according to physical joint connections, and self-loops are explicitly included:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{A}_1[i,j] = \mathbf{A}_1[j,i] =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } (i,j) \in \varepsilon \text{ or } i=j, \\
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon$ denotes the set of physically connected joint pairs.

To allow flexible modeling beyond predefined anatomy, we introduce a learnable adjacency matrix $\mathbf{A}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times J}$, initialized as a uniform matrix with values $1/J$ and optimized during training. The final adjacency matrix is obtained via a learnable convex combination:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{A}} = (1 - \sigma\left(\alpha\right))\mathbf{A}_1 + \sigma\left(\alpha\right)\mathbf{A}_2,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ is a learnable scalar and $\sigma(\cdot)$ denotes the sigmoid function. The combined matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ is further normalized to obtain the final propagation matrix $\mathbf{A}$.

Graph convolution is then performed as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{X}_g \mathbf{W},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is a learnable weight matrix. In this work, we employ a single graph convolution layer for spatial refinement. The output $\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{(B \cdot T \cdot V) \times J \times 3}$ is reshaped back to $\mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times V \times J \times 3}$.

To preserve precise joint coordinates,The LGC module is integrated into the network through a residual design to preserve precise 3D joint coordinates:
 
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_{enhanced} = \mathbf{X}_{in} + \lambda \cdot LayerNorm\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is a learnable scalar parameter and LayerNorm is applied along the coordinate dimension. The enhanced features $\mathbf{X}_{enhanced}$ are subsequently passed to the proposed Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing (JATS) module. By strengthening spatial joint interactions prior to joint-adaptive temporal refinement, this design establishes a robust spatial foundation for effective global temporal reasoning.

\subsection{Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing}

To enhance temporal consistency while preserving joint-specific motion characteristics, we introduce a Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing (JATS) module prior to the Transformer encoder. This module explicitly enforces short-term temporal coherence while allowing different joints to retain distinct motion dynamics.Specifically, the proposed JATS module operates independently on each joint and its corresponding 3D coordinate channels, enabling fine-grained temporal refinement at the joint level while preserving coordinate-specific motion patterns.

Given a 3D joint sequence $\mathbf X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times V \times J \times 3}$, the input features are rearranged along the temporal dimension and reshaped into a channel-first representation, $\mathbf X' \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times T}$ where $\quad C = V \cdot J \cdot 3$.

We then apply a lightweight depth-wise temporal convolution along the temporal axis, where each joint-coordinate channel is processed independently. This design enables efficient local temporal filtering without introducing cross-joint interference, making the module suitable as a pre-processing step before global temporal modeling.

To adaptively control the strength of temporal smoothing for different joints, we introduce a learnable joint-wise and coordinate-aware gating mechanism. The temporally refined features are combined with the original input through a residual formulation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf X_{\text{out}} = \mathbf X' + \Gamma \odot Conv\left(\mathbf X'\right),
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times C \times 1}$ denotes a set of learnable joint-wise and coordinate-aware gates, and $\odot$ represents element-wise multiplication.

Each gate corresponds to a specific joint-coordinate channel, allowing the model to adaptively balance between raw joint trajectories and temporally smoothed signals. More stable joints tend to benefit from stronger smoothing, while highly dynamic joints preserve higher-frequency motion patterns. This adaptive mechanism is fully differentiable and optimized end-to-end with the overall network.

Finally, the gated features are reshaped back to the original spatiotemporal format and fed into the embedding layer and Transformer encoder for global temporal reasoning. The proposed JATS module introduces minimal computational overhead, yet provides an effective inductive bias for modeling heterogeneous joint dynamics, complementing the long-range temporal modeling capability of the Transformer.





\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Dataset}
The SELF dataset~\cite{b1} is a large-scale benchmark for egocentric 3D human pose estimation, capturing multimodal data from 14 subjects. Each subject performs two recording sessions with varied clothing to enhance visual diversity. Each session begins with a short calibration stage, followed by 50 scripted actions (e.g., sports and daily activities), each lasting approximately 20 seconds with short pauses in between, covering a wide range of motion patterns. During recording, participants interact naturally with their surroundings while wearing VR headsets equipped with forward-facing cameras.

The dataset provides stereo egocentric videos, 6D device poses, markerless full-body motion capture, and synchronized multi-view recordings from a 120-camera studio, totaling over 7 hours of data (approximately 1.6 million images). A separate test set containing two additional subjects is provided for unbiased evaluation, contributing around 1 hour of data. For each frame, the dataset includes dual fisheye images $I_t \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 3 \times H \times W}$, floor-aligned device poses $T_D(t) \in SE(3)$, and ground-truth 3D joint positions $J_t \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times 3}$. These rich annotations enable reliable training and evaluation for egocentric 3D pose estimation.


\subsection{Metrics}
Following prior works, we evaluate all methods using the mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) in millimeters. In addition, we report Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE) to assess structural alignment accuracy, and the 3D percentage of correct keypoints (3D-PCK) measured within a 10 cm distance threshold.


\subsection{Implementation Details}
All models are trained using the AdamW optimizer with a batch size of 16. During training, an $\ell_2$ loss is applied to supervise the predicted 3D joint coordinates. We adopt a two-stage training strategy following prior work. In the first stage, the network is optimized in camera coordinates. In the second stage, the temporal Transformer module is further trained using global (world) coordinates to enhance temporal consistency and global pose accuracy. The learning rate for the Transformer is set to $3 \times 10^{-4}$, and training is conducted for 4 epochs.


\subsection{Comparison}
We compare the proposed method with multiple representative egocentric 3D human pose estimation approaches, including the baseline FRAME~\cite{b1} and several prior works, under the same evaluation protocol. All methods are evaluated locally using their officially released pretrained models in a unified testing environment to ensure a fair comparison.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab1}, the proposed method consistently achieves superior or competitive accuracy compared with existing approaches. In particular, our method demonstrates clear improvements over the baseline and prior methods, indicating more accurate and stable 3D pose estimation under challenging egocentric conditions. These results suggest that explicitly strengthening spatial joint relationships and joint-aware local temporal modeling effectively enhances pose estimation performance.

In terms of efficiency, the proposed framework maintains inference latency comparable to the baseline model, incurring only marginal additional computational overhead. Despite introducing extra spatial and temporal refinement modules, the overall runtime remains within the real-time range, demonstrating that the proposed design is lightweight and practical for deployment.

Overall, the comparison results show that the proposed method achieves a favorable balance between accuracy and efficiency, providing a practical improvement over existing egocentric 3D human pose estimation approaches without sacrificing real-time performance.

\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we presented an enhanced spatiotemporal framework for egocentric 3D human pose estimation. By explicitly strengthening spatial and temporal representations, our approach effectively addresses key challenges in egocentric settings, such as severe self-occlusion and complex pose dynamics. Specifically, we introduced a Learnable Graph Convolution module that integrates anatomical priors with data-driven graph learning to refine spatial joint relationships while preserving precise 3D coordinates. In addition, we proposed a Joint-wise Adaptive Temporal Smoothing module that performs lightweight, joint-level temporal refinement prior to transformer-based global temporal reasoning.Extensive experiments and ablation studies demonstrate that each component contributes positively, enabling the full model to consistently outperform multiple existing methods across standard evaluation metrics. Importantly, the proposed spatial and temporal enhancements introduce only marginal computational overhead, resulting in negligible impact on inference latency and preserving real-time performance. Overall, this work highlights the importance of explicitly disentangling and reinforcing spatial and local temporal modeling in egocentric 3D human pose estimation, and provides an effective and efficient extension to transformer-based frameworks.
